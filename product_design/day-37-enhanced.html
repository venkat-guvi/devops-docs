1. Introduction to Business Thinking in Design

When we say business thinking in design, we are talking about a mindset shift.

Most beginner designers think their job is:

Making screens look attractive

Choosing colors and typography

Creating layouts

But in real-world products, design is not just about visuals — it is about impact.

A digital product exists for a reason:

Either to generate revenue

Or to support a business goal (like growth, engagement, retention)

So a designer must start asking:

What is this product trying to achieve financially?

How does this screen contribute to that goal?

Designer Thinking: Before vs After

Beginner Designer Thinks

Business-Aware Designer Thinks

How does this look?

How does this convert?

Is this visually appealing?

Does this support revenue?

Which color palette fits?

What user action does this drive?

Is the layout balanced?

Does this reduce friction to purchase?

Example: Amazon Product Page

Amazon — Every element is intentionally placed:

Cart button -> drives purchase

Buy Now -> reduces steps to conversion

Recommended products -> increases average order value

Reviews & ratings -> builds trust, reduces hesitation

That is business thinking in design.

7. Key Business Metrics

These metrics help measure the success of a digital product.

Metric

Full Name

What It Means

Design Relevance

Revenue

—

Total money earned by the product

Drive more revenue events

Profit

—

Revenue minus expenses

Reduce friction, improve conversion

CAC

Customer Acquisition Cost

Cost to acquire one new user

Lower CAC by improving onboarding

LTV

Lifetime Value

Total revenue from one user over time

Improve retention screens

Churn

—

% of users who stop using/paying

Reduce cancellation friction

📌
09 / 15

DAU/MAU

Daily / Monthly Active Users

Engagement ratio

Build habits and return triggers

🔑 Key Insight:

If CAC > LTV -> The business loses money and will fail.

If LTV > CAC -> The business grows profitably.

Designers influence LTV by improving retention, engagement, and trust.

10. Dark Patterns vs Ethical Monetization

Dark Patterns ❌

Ethical Monetization ✅

Hidden charges at checkout

Transparent pricing upfront

Forced subscriptions (impossible cancel)

Clear, easy cancellation flow

Misleading buttons (roach motels)

Honest, clear call-to-action labels

Disguised ads as content

Clearly labelled sponsored content

Fake urgency / countdown timers

Real time-sensitive offers only

Pre-ticked boxes for extra charges

Opt-in by default, not opt-out

✅ Ethical design builds long-term success.

Trust is the foundation of sustainable revenue.
