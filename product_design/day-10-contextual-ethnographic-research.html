01 Contextual Inquiry -Real- World Observation

📌
03 / 11

What is Contextual Inquiry?

Observing users in their natural environment while they perform real tasks.

Real Scenario

Priya, a 28-year-old software developer, shops on Flipkart while commuting on the metro. Her session is rushed — she has 8 minutes before her stop. She squints at product photos, repeatedly taps the back button by mistake, and gives up on filtering.

📌
04 / 11

OBSERVATION

Misclicked navigation multiple times

Difficulty reading product details

Interrupted task flow due to time pressure

Dropped off before completing filters

📌
05 / 11

OBSERVATION

Insight: Observing > Asking. In an interview, Priya would say 'I find the app easy.' In the field, you see the truth.

02 Ethnographic Research Fundamentals

📌
06 / 11

What is Ethnographic Research?

Studying users in their daily life environment to understand behavior patterns.

Real Scenario

Raj, 34, shops online from his couch at 10 PM. The TV is on, his laptop has 6 tabs open, and he switches between his phone and laptop to compare prices. His behavior is shaped entirely by his environment — not product design.

📌
07 / 11

OBSERVATION

Frequent device switching

Distracted browsing behavior

Adds items without intent to purchase

Insight: Behavior is shaped by the environment. Users don't behave in your app — they behave in their life.

03 Field Studies — Research in Real Locations

What are Field Studies?

Conducting research in the actual location where the product is used.

Real Scenario

Suresh, 55, is at a State Bank ATM in Bangalore. The sun glare makes the screen hard to read. He spends 40 seconds searching for the 'Cancel' button. He nearly enters his PIN on the wrong screen.

📌
08 / 11

OBSERVATION

Screen visibility issues due to glare

Hesitation before each action

Misinterpretation of button labels

Insight: Real environments expose hidden problems. The ATM worked fine in QA testing. In the sun, in a crowd, it fails real users.

04 Capturing Behaviors & Pain Points

Pain points don't announce themselves. They appear in hesitations, repeated taps, wrong paths taken, and sudden exits. The researcher's job is to capture these micro-behaviors.

Key Behaviors to Watch For

Hesitation before tapping.

Back navigation when unexpected.

Repeated the same action.

Long gaze before deciding.

Abandonment mid-flow.

Real Scenario

Meera is checking out on Amazon. The promo code field is hidden below the fold. She taps the Pay button 4 times before realizing the promo wasn't applied. She goes back, loses her cart, and exits the app frustrated.

📌
09 / 11

OBSERVATION

Multiple taps on wrong button

Missed promo code section

Navigated back and lost progress

Exited without completing purchase

Insight: Pain points are visible in actions, not words. No user says 'I keep tapping the wrong button.' But they do it every time.

05 Challenges in Real-World Research

What are the Challenges?

Uncontrolled conditions that affect research quality and flow.

Common Challenges

User distraction mid-session.

The researcher loses line of sight.

Privacy concerns block recording.

Noise corrupts audio notes.

Users change behavior when observed (Hawthorne Effect).

Real Scenario

During a field study at a Reliance Fresh store, the researcher loses the participant in a crowded aisle. The user's phone call interrupts the task. The researcher can't note observations without being noticed.

📌
10 / 11

OBSERVATION

Participants moved unpredictably.

Session interrupted by phone call.

Difficulty in capturing notes.

Limited ability to record.

Insight: Real-world research is messy — but that mess is your most valuable data. The interruptions are part of the user's real experience.

06 Converting Observations into Insights

What does this mean?

Turning raw observations into patterns and actionable insights.

The Transformation Process

Observation → Group into themes → Find patterns → Formulate insight → Map to design opportunity

Real Scenario

After 5 field sessions across different Flipkart users, the team has 60+ sticky notes. Clustering them reveals: 3 distinct navigation patterns, 2 major checkout friction clusters, and 1 trust gap. Each cluster becomes a design brief.

📌
11 / 11

OBSERVATION

Repeated navigation issues

Checkout friction points

Trust-related concerns

Insight: Observation → Pattern → Insight → Design Decision. Every sticky note is evidence. Every cluster is a story waiting to become a better product.
