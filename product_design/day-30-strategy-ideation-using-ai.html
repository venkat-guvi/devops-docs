AI PROMPT TEMPLATES FOR BRAINSTORMING

Feature Ideas

"List 10 innovative features for [product] that would help [user type] achieve [goal]"

Gets diverse functional ideas quickly

Useful for early-stage exploration

UX Improvements

"What are 8 UX improvements for the [screen/flow] in a [product type] app?"

Surfaces friction points and enhancements

Works best with a specific screen or flow in mind

Accessibility Ideas

"How can [feature] be made more accessible for users with [disability type]?"

Uncovers inclusion opportunities that are often missed

Helps you design for the full spectrum of users

User Pain Points

"What are the top frustrations users face when [doing task] in [context]?"

Reveals hidden problem areas

Best when combined with real user research findings

HOW TO USE AI FOR DIVERGENT THINKING

🌊 Go Wide First

Ask AI for 15–20 ideas without filtering

Quantity before quality — let the breadth surprise you

🔀 Explore Opposites

Prompt AI with contradictory directions

"What if we removed the feature entirely?" or "What's the most radical solution?"

🎯 Then Converge

After mapping many directions, bring the best 3–4 to the team

Evaluate, vote, and refine with your team

THREE IDEA CATEGORIES

✅ Strong Idea — One-Tap Reorder

Solves a frequent, high-frustration task

Easy to build, measurable impact on repeat purchase rate

→ Decision: Prioritize → Build

❌ Weak Idea — Blockchain Receipts

No clear user need — users don't understand or care

Extremely high complexity with no measurable user benefit

→ Decision: Discard → Move On

⚠️ Needs Validation — AI Chat on All Screens

Potentially useful but completely unvalidated

Could add cognitive load and confusion

→ Decision: Test → Then Decide

AI is powerful but dangerous when used thoughtlessly. These are the five mistakes junior designers make most often — and how to avoid them.

THE 5 COMMON MISTAKES

MISTAKE BREAKDOWN

🤖 Mistake 1 · Over-Relying on AI

Treating AI as the source of truth instead of a starting point

Impact: You design for AI's worldview, not real users' needs

→ Fix: Always pair AI outputs with user research and real behavioral data

🔬 Mistake 2 · No User Validation

Building AI-generated features without testing with real users first

Impact: You ship features users never asked for and don't need

→ Fix: Prototype and test the top 3 ideas with at least 5 real users

🌊 Mistake 3 · Too Many Ideas, No Priority

Collecting 50 AI ideas without any framework for deciding what to pursue

Impact: Team paralysis — nothing gets built because everything is "possible.ible"

→ Fix: Use an impact vs. effort matrix to cluster and prioritize after ideation

♿ Mistake 4 · Ignoring Accessibility

Accepting AI feature ideas without checking if they're accessible by design

Impact: You exclude entire user groups from your experience

→ Fix: Always add "make this accessible for users with [disability]" to your AI prompts

📋 Mistake 5 · Copying AI Outputs Directly

Pasting AI-generated UX copy, flows, or concepts into deliverables as-is

Impact: Generic, soulless experiences that lack brand voice and user context

→ Fix: Always edit, adapt, and inject real user language and brand personality

DO'S AND DON'TS AT A GLANCE

✗ Don't

Ask AI one question and ship the first answer

Skip user research because "AI already knows the answer."

Copy the AI-generated UX copy word for word

Prioritize by what AI generated the most of

Let AI define the scope of what's possible

Ignore the context, AI wasn't given

✓ Do

Use AI as a starting point, then iterate with users

Pair AI brainstorms with qualitative research insights

Rewrite AI outputs in your product's voice and tone

Use a structured framework to prioritize ideas

Push AI with constraints: budget, time, tech limitations

Add the context AI is missing: culture, emotion, edge cases

Blindly Accepting AI Outputs

AI is confident by design — but confidence isn't accuracy. The best designers use AI as raw material, not final deliverables.

THE FOUR VALIDATION PILLARS

🧠 Critical Thinking

Every AI idea deserves a "why would a real user care about this?" question

Don't ship based on intuition — verify with data

👤 Human Judgment

AI lacks cultural context, emotional nuance, and lived experience

Designers bring this irreplaceable layer to every decision

🔬 User Research

Real user data always trumps AI-generated assumptions

Validate with interviews, usability tests, and analytics

🔄 Iteration Loop

AI-generated ideas are hypotheses, not solutions

Design is the process of testing and refining hypotheses with real humans

THE 7 - PHASE PROCESS TIMELINE

📌
29 / 31

PHASE-BY-PHASE BREAKDOWN

Research

Ground ideation in real data before touching AI

User interviews — 5–8 participants minimum

Analytics review — identify drop-off points

Competitor benchmarking and desk research

Affinity mapping of research findings

AI Brainstorming

With the research context loaded, use AI to generate ideas rapidly

Feed AI: research insights + product constraints

Generate 20–30 initial concepts across directions

Ask for HMW reframes of key research insights

Generate detailed user scenarios for edge cases

Divergent Exploration

Don't filter too early — explore multiple directions

Cluster ideas into 4–6 strategic directions

Explore each direction with deeper AI prompts

Ask AI for radical alternatives and opposites

Prioritization

Apply structured frameworks to select the top directions

Impact vs. effort matrix for objective scoring

Dot voting with the team for alignment

Select the top 3 directions for prototyping

Refinement

Work the top ideas into proper UX flows and prototypes

Wireframes → mid-fidelity prototypes

AI-assisted UX copy generation and variation

Edge case and error state design

Testing

Put prototypes in front of real users — observe, listen, capture

Usability testing — 5+ users minimum

Task completion measurement

Emotional response observation and note-taking

Iteration

Incorporate test findings and refine until the design is earned

Synthesize test feedback into clear design changes

AI-assisted micro-iteration for targeted improvements

Ship → measure → learn → repeat the cycle
